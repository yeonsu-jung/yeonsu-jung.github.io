Summary

Mechanical engineer (Ph.D.) and Research Associate at Harvard School of Engineering and Applied Sciences. I work on contact-rich and deformable systems in three ways: physics-based simulation and the numerical optimization that runs on it; X-ray micro-CT and 3D image analysis, where I build the fixtures, run the scanners and write the analysis; and experiments. What I most want to work on is real-to-sim-to-real transfer: a simulator built from measurements of the real system, calibrated against hardware, and results that hold when they go back to it. I run multiple projects of more than 100 commits each with AI coding and science agents (Claude Code, Codex), every step gated by reproduction and conformance checks. Co-author of 14 peer-reviewed journal papers, five of them in PNAS, and others in Science Robotics and Science Advances.

Selected projects

Entangled rod packings: optimization, contact engines, X-ray CT2021–2026 · PNAS 2025 · PRL under revision

  • Wrote a JAX optimizer (FIRE, on GPU) that maximizes the entanglement of up to 2,000 rods without overlap, and a C++17 engine for rigid rods in contact (cone complementarity by projected Gauss–Seidel, soft contact, OpenMP, a CUDA broad phase).
  • Showed that maximally entangled packings cage themselves and hold together by friction alone. Re-ran the dynamics in MuJoCo to test which results survive a change of contact model: the cooling law does (time constant within 15%), contact lifetimes do not.
  • Measured real steel-rod packings by X-ray micro-CT: built the fixtures, ran the scans, and wrote a pipeline that traces about 4,600 rods in a 2000 × 2000 × 741 voxel scan in 12 minutes.

X-ray micro-CT and 3D image analysis2021–2026 · eLife 2025

  • Scan metal, polymer, gel, granular and biological samples on two systems, and write the software that turns the scans into measurements. A browser viewer ray-marches five of the tomograms on the GPU.
  • Building in-situ load frames for both scanners (ongoing): force is logged while a packing is scanned at each load step, and each scan gives its contacts and the contact network's algebraic connectivity.
  • When scans on a shared scanner began to show out-of-focus halos, ruled out the sample with a series of test scans and showed that the X-ray beam and the detector were misaligned; the service company then fixed it on site.

Elastic rods in joint coordinates, for robot simulators2025–2026

  • Derived one swing–twist rod energy with an exact ball-joint torque, implemented four ways (NumPy, JAX, PyTorch in mjlab, a C++ MuJoCo plugin) that agree to 2×10−11. Found that MuJoCo's stock cable torque is not the gradient of an energy (off by 7.7–46.7% once the bend axis tilts).
  • Control on the verified model, in simulation: a whip crack optimized by differentiating through MJX (tip at 68 m/s, 5.8 mm from its target); a periodic LQR; PPO in 4,096 parallel environments (MuJoCo Warp) that rears a 16-link chain; receding-horizon control that rights a tumbling glider in 10 of 10 launches.

Models of collaborators' experiments2022–2026 · PNAS 2022 · PNAS 2026

  • Calibrated a simulator of centimeter-scale vibration-driven robot chains against hardware: it matches 23 of 30 cells of the measured stability map. I rejected a variant that fitted better but stretched the joints beyond their measured play.
  • Predicted how rotationally 3D-printed lattices morph when heated, with discrete elastic rods, and fitted designs to target surfaces in JAX (Lewis group); analysis and micro-CT of an entanglement gripper (Wood group; reproduced N. Charles's simulation).

Experiments

  • Swallow nests (PNAS 2021, first author): measured the strength of real nests and of polymer-bridged granules in three-point bending and in a centrifuge, printed nests from clay and mucin, and computed the stress in a 3D-scanned nest by finite elements (COMSOL): it peaks where a real nest tore away.
  • Hydrogel beads on a hot pan (Physical Review E, under revision, corresponding author): devised the Leidenfrost experiment with M. Cho and J. Ha, in which a bead gets its energy only from vapor bursts at the wall, and model its motion.
  • Earlier: artificial roots drawing water from packed beads (J. R. Soc. Interface 2019); irregular cylinders rolling down a ramp, tracked on video (PNAS 2025).

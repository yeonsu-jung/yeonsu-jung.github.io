# Shark-Skin-Inspired Surfaces for Drag Reduction

*Fabrication and fluid-mechanical testing of textured surfaces, inspired by shark denticles.*

---

## Motivation

Shark skin is covered in microscopic riblet-like denticles that are believed to reduce drag. At the Rowland Institute at Harvard (2020–2021, with Dr. Shabnam Raayai-Ardakani), I studied how engineered surface textures modify skin-friction drag — a problem where careful experimental design matters as much as the physics.

## What I did

- Designed and fabricated textured surfaces via mold design and polymer casting
- Built experimental setups to measure drag on textured versus smooth surfaces under controlled flow conditions
- Analyzed the measurements to connect texture geometry (spacing, height, shape) to drag performance
- Used the resulting data for physics-guided optimization of the surface geometry

## Why it matters

Drag reduction from surface texture is a delicate, geometry-sensitive effect: the same riblets that help at one scale can hurt at another. Quantifying this reliably requires tight tolerances in fabrication, well-characterized flow, and honest statistics — an end-to-end exercise in experimental mechanics.

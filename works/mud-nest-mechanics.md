# Mechanics of Avian Mud Nests

*How swallows build cantilevered homes from mud and saliva — granular composites tested to failure.*

---

## Motivation

Barn swallows build nests from thousands of mud pellets bonded by self-secreted saliva, cantilevered off vertical walls, supporting many times the birds' own weight. What makes this granular composite strong enough? ([PNAS 2021](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2018509118))

## What I did

- Field and lab characterization of real nest material: composition, pellet structure, and porosity
- Mechanical testing of saliva- and polymer-bonded granular composites, measuring strength as a function of binder content and moisture
- Physical modeling of the bonding mechanism — capillarity-assisted adhesion and the drying-induced strengthening of the pellet assembly
- Analysis connecting the nest's construction sequence (pellet-by-pellet deposition) to its final load-bearing capacity

## Why it matters

The nest is a natural example of *additive manufacturing with wet granular matter*: material deposition, curing, and structural function are inseparable. The same physics — capillary bridges, drying, particle-binder mechanics — appears in 3D printing of dense suspensions and in construction robotics. This work was featured in [Harvard SEAS news](https://seas.harvard.edu/news/2025/04/how-do-bird-nests-stay-together) and Korean national media.
